How to calculate your true hourly pay from gig work

A driver who sees $25 an hour in the app can easily be keeping half that. Four numbers tell you the truth, and once you know them you'll make better decisions about which offers to take.

By the Cash.Work editorial team. Updated November 19, 2026. 6 min read.

Step 1: Count every logged-in hour

Apps often highlight "active" time: the minutes between accepting an order and dropping it off. But the time you spend waiting for the next request, driving to a busy area or sitting outside a restaurant is still time you can't spend elsewhere. Count from when you log in to when you log out.

Step 2: Add up everything you were paid

Take base pay, promotions, bonuses and tips for the same period. Weekly totals are more reliable than single days, because one great Friday can hide three slow weekdays.

Step 3: Subtract the cost of working

For drivers, the big one is the vehicle. Record your odometer at the start and end of every shift, including the drive to your first order. Then multiply by a realistic cost per mile or kilometre:

  • Fuel or charging: what you pay at the pump divided by the distance you get from it.
  • Maintenance and tyres: oil changes, brakes and tyres wear faster with gig driving.
  • Depreciation: every kilometre reduces what your car is worth.
  • Insurance: some insurers require a rideshare or business-use add-on.

Tax authorities publish standard mileage or kilometre rates each year that bundle these costs together. They're a useful benchmark for the full cost of running a car, even if you don't use them for your tax return. Add other costs too: phone data, parking, insulated bags, tolls and platform fees that aren't already deducted.

Step 4: Set aside tax

Gig platforms usually treat you as self-employed, so no tax is taken off your pay. Put a percentage of every payout aside. Read our gig worker tax basics for what that covers in the US, Canada and the UK.

A worked example

ItemWeek total
Earnings and tips shown in the app$520
Logged-in hours26
Distance driven: 410 miles at $0.30 (fuel and maintenance)−$123
Phone, parking and supplies−$20
Tax set-aside at 20% of $377−$75
Kept$302

The app suggests $20 an hour ($520 / 26). The real figure is about $11.60 an hour ($302 / 26), and lower again if you include depreciation and insurance. That gap is normal, and knowing it is what lets you improve it. Run your own numbers in the gig pay calculator.

How to raise the number

  • Set a minimum pay per mile and decline offers below it. Long trips for low pay drag your average down more than anything else.
  • Work peak windows. Meal times and weekend evenings usually bring more orders and higher promotions.
  • Cut dead miles. Wait near busy restaurant clusters instead of driving around.
  • Consider a cheaper vehicle. In dense cities, an e-bike can beat a car on cost per hour.
  • Compare platforms by real pay, not by their marketing. Track a week on each before you commit.

This guide is general information, not financial, tax or legal advice. Platforms change their pay and rules often; check the official site before you sign up.
