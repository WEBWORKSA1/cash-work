Gig worker taxes: the basics in the US, Canada and the UK

Delivery, rideshare and freelance platforms almost never withhold tax. That makes you responsible for it. These are the essentials, so tax season doesn't wipe out a year of side income.

By the Cash.Work editorial team. Updated November 19, 2026. 8 min read.

You're probably self-employed

Most gig platforms treat workers as independent contractors. That means three things: no tax is withheld from your pay, you can usually deduct legitimate business expenses, and you report the income yourself on your tax return. A few shift platforms hire people as employees instead and withhold tax for you; check your contract or pay stub to know which you are.

The habit that solves most problems

Open a separate savings account and move a fixed share of every payout into it the day you're paid. Many self-employed people start at 20–30%, then adjust once they know their real rate. When the tax bill arrives, the money is already there.

Track these from day one

  • Income: every payout from every platform, plus tips and cash jobs.
  • Mileage or kilometres driven for work, with dates. A simple log or tracking app is enough.
  • Expenses: phone and data share, supplies, platform fees, parking, tolls, a portion of vehicle costs, software and equipment for freelance work.
  • Receipts: photograph them; keep records for as long as your tax authority requires.

United States

  • Self-employed people pay self-employment tax for Social Security and Medicare (15.3% on most net earnings) on top of income tax.
  • If you expect to owe $1,000 or more for the year, the IRS expects quarterly estimated tax payments. Missing them can mean penalties.
  • Platforms may send you a Form 1099-NEC or 1099-K, but you owe tax on all self-employment income whether or not you receive a form.
  • Drivers can generally deduct either actual vehicle expenses or the IRS standard mileage rate, which is published each year.
  • Income and expenses are reported on Schedule C, with self-employment tax on Schedule SE.

Canada

  • Report self-employment income and expenses on form T2125 with your return.
  • You pay both the employee and employer portions of CPP contributions on self-employment earnings.
  • GST/HST: most self-employed people must register once their taxable sales pass $30,000 over four consecutive quarters. Rideshare drivers are different: they must register for GST/HST from their first fare. Food delivery drivers follow the normal $30,000 small-supplier rule.
  • If you owe more than a set amount at tax time, the CRA may ask you to pay by instalments the following year.
  • Quebec residents also report to Revenu Québec and contribute to the QPP instead of the CPP.

United Kingdom

  • The trading allowance lets you earn up to £1,000 a year from self-employment tax-free. Above that, register for Self Assessment with HMRC.
  • You'll pay income tax and Class 4 National Insurance on profits above the relevant thresholds.
  • Digital platforms now report sellers' and gig workers' income to HMRC, so it's worth keeping your own records that match.

India and elsewhere

Freelance and gig income is generally taxable as business or professional income. In India, eligible professionals may be able to use presumptive taxation, and GST registration can apply above certain turnover. Rules vary widely by country, so check your national tax authority's guidance for self-employed people.

When to get help

If you earn a meaningful share of your income from gigs, a one-time session with a tax professional usually pays for itself. Bring your income totals, mileage log and expense receipts. Our gig pay calculator can help you estimate how much to set aside in the meantime.

This guide is general information, not financial, tax or legal advice. Platforms change their pay and rules often; check the official site before you sign up.
