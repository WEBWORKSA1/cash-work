The lowest rate that still pays your bills

Start with the income you want to take home and work backwards. Most new freelancers underprice because they forget unbillable hours.

Why billable hours matter most

A 40-hour week as a freelancer rarely means 40 invoiced hours. Finding clients, writing proposals, admin, learning and revisions you can't bill can easily take a third of your time. If you only bill 25 hours a week for 46 weeks, that's 1,150 hours a year, not 2,080.

The calculator grosses up your take-home goal for tax, adds business costs, divides by realistic billable hours and adds a safety margin for late payments and quiet months.

Using the number

  • Treat the result as a floor. Quote above it when the client's budget or the value of the work allows.
  • For fixed-price projects, estimate hours generously and multiply by your rate.
  • Platform fees reduce what you receive. If a marketplace keeps 10–20%, raise your listed rate to compensate.
