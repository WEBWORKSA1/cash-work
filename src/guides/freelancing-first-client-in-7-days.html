Freelancing: how to land your first client in seven days

Your first freelance client is the hardest one, mostly because the offer is too vague. This plan narrows it to one clear service, a fair price and two places to find buyers, so you can start getting replies within a week.

By the Cash.Work editorial team. Updated November 19, 2026. 7 min read.

Day 1: Choose one service you can deliver this week

Not "marketing" or "design": something specific a buyer can picture. Writing five product descriptions. Editing a short video. Setting up a booking page. Cleaning a spreadsheet. Translating a menu. Pick a service you've done before, even unpaid, and can finish in a few hours.

Day 2: Package and price it

Turn the service into a fixed package with a clear deliverable, a delivery time and a price. Offer three sizes if it makes sense: basic, standard and complete. Work out a floor price with the freelance rate calculator. For a first client, pricing slightly under the market to get a review is fine; pricing at almost nothing attracts the wrong buyers and teaches you nothing.

Day 3: Build proof

If you don't have a portfolio, make one sample that shows exactly what the client would get. Rewrite a real company's weak product page (without claiming it was commissioned), redesign a local shop's flyer, or clean a public dataset. Two strong samples beat ten mediocre ones.

Day 4: Set up two storefronts

Create a profile on one marketplace that fits your service, such as Upwork for project bids, Fiverr for fixed packages or Contra for a commission-free portfolio. Then set up a simple one-page portfolio you control. Write your headline as the outcome you deliver: "Product descriptions that make browsers buy", not "Freelance writer".

Day 5: Pitch ten times

On marketplaces, apply only to posts where you match the brief closely, and reply within hours of posting. Open with the client's problem, link your relevant sample, and suggest a small first step. Outside marketplaces, contact ten small businesses whose website or social media obviously needs your service, with one specific improvement you'd make.

Day 6: Follow up and adjust

Look at what got a reply. Tighten your headline, sharpen your samples, and send a polite follow-up to anyone who opened the conversation. Most first clients come from the second message, not the first.

Day 7: Deliver early and ask for a review

When you land the job, confirm the scope in writing, deliver before the deadline and ask for a review and a referral. A five-star review on your first marketplace job makes the next ten pitches much easier.

Money basics from day one

  • Use the platform's escrow or take a deposit for direct clients.
  • Remember marketplace fees when you set your price.
  • Set aside part of every payment for tax; see tax basics for self-employed workers.
