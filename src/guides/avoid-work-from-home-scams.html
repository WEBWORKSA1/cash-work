How to spot and avoid work-from-home job scams

Job scams target exactly the people this site is for: anyone who needs income soon. The good news is that almost every scam breaks one of a few simple rules. Learn them once and you'll spot the rest.

By the Cash.Work editorial team. Updated November 19, 2026. 7 min read.

The golden rule

Real jobs pay you. They never ask you to pay them. No legitimate employer or gig platform asks for money to apply, train, buy a starter kit, run a background check you arrange yourself, or "unlock" higher-paying work.

The five scams you're most likely to meet

1. The fake cheque

You're "hired" and sent a cheque to buy equipment from a recommended vendor or to cover costs, then asked to send the extra back. The cheque bounces days later, after your bank has made the funds available, and you owe the full amount.

2. The task scam

A recruiter messages you about simple online work: rating products, liking videos or "optimising apps". You earn small amounts at first. Then you're told to deposit money, often in crypto, to unlock bigger tasks or withdraw your balance. The balance was never real.

3. The chat-app interview

The "interview" happens entirely in Telegram, WhatsApp or Signal, often with a well-known company's name attached. Real companies interview by phone or video, with named people you can look up.

4. Reshipping and payment processing

You receive packages to forward abroad, or payments to move through your bank account. These "jobs" often involve stolen goods or money laundering, and you can be held responsible.

5. Pay-to-start schemes

Training fees, certification fees, starter kits and "membership" to see job listings. Some of these are outright scams; others are multi-level marketing dressed up as a job.

How to verify an employer in five minutes

  1. Find the company's own website by searching for it, not by clicking links in the message. Look for the job on its careers page.
  2. Check that emails come from the company's domain, not a free email address.
  3. Search the company name with "scam" or "reviews" and look for patterns.
  4. Look up the recruiter on a professional network and confirm they actually work there.
  5. If something feels off, contact the company through the phone number or email on its official site and ask whether the offer is real.

What legitimate hiring looks like

  • A clear job description with pay stated up front.
  • An interview with real people, by phone, video or in person.
  • A written offer before you're asked for bank or tax details.
  • Personal and banking information collected through a proper HR or payroll system, not a chat message.

If you've already been targeted

  1. Stop responding. Don't send any more money, gift card codes or documents.
  2. Call your bank immediately if you shared account details or deposited a cheque.
  3. Report it: in the US at ReportFraud.ftc.gov, in Canada to the Canadian Anti-Fraud Centre, in the UK to Action Fraud, and in India on the National Cyber Crime Reporting Portal. Also report the ad or profile on the site where you found it.
  4. If you shared ID numbers, follow your country's identity-theft guidance and consider a credit freeze or fraud alert.
